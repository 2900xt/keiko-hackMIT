1. **Blocker — hydrophone posts are floating islands.** `head_body()` places each post at radius 11 mm with Ø3.5 mm (`L142`), while the sinker ring’s inner radius is 13 mm (`L140`). Each post reaches only 12.75 mm: a 0.25 mm air gap. They begin at z=11 mm over the empty sinker pocket, so the slicer will attempt to print three unsupported, disconnected columns. Fix: add radial ribs from the posts to the ring, or move/resize them to overlap it substantially; e.g. use `cylinder(d=4.5)` centered at radius 12 mm, plus a 2.4 mm-wide radial rib to the ring.

2. **Blocker — the head’s top eye begins in mid-air.** The eye is a horizontal-axis ring centered at z=50 mm (`L144`), extending down to about z=43 mm. The head’s interior is void up to z=47 mm (`L127`), so its first ~4 mm prints with nothing beneath it. The three-spoke roof also starts as a roughly 39.2 mm-diameter ceiling (`L127`, `L131–133`), which is an unreliable bridge at 0.28 mm, especially with the eye already failing below it. Fix: replace the eye with a vertical-axis annular tab built upward from the roof, and make the roof a support-free cone/spokes rising at ≤45°, or print a separate eye flat and mechanically attach it.

3. **Blocker — the lid’s 60 × 60 mm “recess” is a 60 mm bridge in its specified orientation.** The lid is intended outer-top-down (`L96`), but the solar recess removes the first 1.6 mm over a 60 mm square (`L107`). At z=1.6 mm the printer must span that whole square with no material below; the nearby cable boss does not solve it. This will sag badly or fail. Fix: delete the recessed solid panel for this print orientation and use an engraved outline/raised graphic instead; otherwise enable support and accept poor removability/finish in the recess.

4. **Blocker — the upper mooring lug does not print flat.** The two lower lugs at z=0 do (`L74`), but the third call is `eye_lug(106, 90)` (`L75`). Its 6 mm-thick horizontal lower face projects from the curved hull wall at z=106 mm, with no support below. The helper’s “prints flat” comment (`L57`) applies only to the bottom calls. Fix: print the top lug as a separate flat part and fasten it, or redesign it as a vertical fin/eye with 45° gussets from the hull.

5. **Blocker — the gasket groove removes the entire neck rim.** With `hull_r=50`, `wall=2.4`, and `groove_w=2.6`, the groove spans radii 47.5–50.1 mm (`L80–84`). The cavity already reaches radius 47.6 mm (`L78`) and the exterior ends at 50 mm. Thus the groove breaks through both sides of the 2.4 mm wall; it is not a retained groove. The exported `hull.stl` confirms its maximum z is **130.6 mm**, not 132 mm: the upper 1.4 mm of the neck wall is gone. Fix: either set `wall=3.6` mm to retain 0.5 mm lands on both sides of the 2.6 mm groove, or reduce `groove_w` to ≤1.4 mm and `groove_d` to about 0.8 mm.

6. **Major — the round helical ridge is not a support-free thread profile.** `helix_ridge()` twists a Ø3 mm circle (`L51–55`); the hull ridge starts abruptly at z=118 mm (`L72`) and produces a near-horizontal lower flank/ledge during upward printing. The matching internal groove has the corresponding difficult upper flank (`L104`). At 0.28 mm layers, a 3 mm round profile is much less forgiving than a 45° thread flank, and the start has no lead-in. Fix: replace the circular profile with a 45° triangular or trapezoidal profile, about 1.0–1.2 mm radial height with a 2.4 mm axial base; add 1–2 mm chamfered lead-ins and a thread-end relief.

7. **Major — 0.35 mm radial thread clearance is marginal for this large PLA screw lid.** The hull crest reaches radius 51.5 mm; the internal groove reaches 51.85 mm, exactly the specified 0.35 mm radial clearance (`L17–20`, `L44–48`, `L104`). That is only 0.70 mm diametral clearance before hole shrinkage, elephant-foot, seam blobs, and the round-flank overhang defects. The 5 mm pitch leaves only 2 mm nominal root-to-root axial space. Fix: use `clr=0.45`–`0.50` mm radial, keep pitch 5 mm, and use the 45° profile above. Deburr the first thread turn before relying on it.

8. **Major — the “under 4 hours” claim is not credible for the hull across Bambu/Prusa-class profiles.** The exported hull is **137.27 cm³ / 170.2 g**, consistent with the source estimate (`L157–163`). It requires a sustained, zero-overhead volumetric average of **9.53 mm³/s** just to finish in four hours:  
   `137,270 mm³ ÷ 14,400 s = 9.53 mm³/s`.  
   That excludes travel, 471-ish layers, outer-wall slowdowns, seam handling, and the thread/lug detail. A fast Bambu profile may approach four hours; a typical Prusa-quality 0.28 mm profile is likely over it. The lid plus head export to **69.07 cm³ / 85.7 g**, so Plate B is plausibly under four hours. Fix: do not schedule the hull as a guaranteed <4 h plate; reduce height/material, use a larger layer height only after thread redesign, or allocate it to the faster printer first.
