1. **Blocker — the drop-line eye will print in mid-air and will be weak even if rescued with support.** At [lines 143–144](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:143), the eye is centered at `z=head_h=50`, radius 7 mm, so its lower arc begins at about `z=43`. The cage interior is empty through `z=47` ([line 127](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:127)); the first ~4 mm of the eye is an unsupported island. Its resulting STL is 57 mm high, confirming the 7 mm protrusion. Supports would be trapped in the cage. Also, the crossed zip-tie cut removes most of the eye’s central attachment.

   Fix: move the eye to the outer rim, with two 3–4 mm thick, gusseted legs that begin on the roof/wall, and put the loop center at `head_h + 7` so the entire loop is above the roof. Keep the 6 mm opening, but do not attach it through the cable/zip-tie boss.

2. **Blocker — the piezo is only balanced on three posts; it is not retained.** The posts run from `z≈11` to `z≈16` ([line 142](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:142)). A nominal 20 mm disc rests on their tops, with only `0.75 mm` radial overlap: post centers are at 11 mm radius, and their inner edges reach 9.25 mm while the disc edge is at 10 mm. There is no upper lip, clamp, or captured seat. A knock, inversion during handling, or tether jerk can lift/tilt the disc off the supports; its lead wires then become the retainer.

   Fix: add a `piezo_pack_d` parameter for the *sealed* assembly, not bare `piezo_d`; use at least 22–24 mm for a balloon/nail-polish envelope. Add three 1.2 mm radial retaining tabs, 0.4–0.6 mm above that assembly, or a removable printed retaining ring. Set post radius to `piezo_pack_d/2 + 1` and retain at least 0.75–1.0 mm radial overlap.

3. **Major — the sinker will rattle, contaminating the hydrophone and defeating TDOA quality.** The ring is an 8 mm-tall, 26 mm-ID pocket ([lines 38, 140](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:38)); ordinary washers/M8 hardware will have substantial radial clearance inside it, and no axial preload. Waves, line motion, and the printed cage will generate metal-on-PLA impacts immediately beside the piezo.

   Fix: set `sinker_d` to the actual stack OD plus only 0.5–0.8 mm, set `sinker_h` to the measured stack height plus 0.3–0.5 mm, and add a printed cap/retainer that also serves as the piezo seat. At minimum, wrap the washer stack tightly in electrical tape before insertion.

4. **Major — the cable is not strain-relieved.** The 5 mm hole is merely a pass-through ([line 135](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:135)). The purported zip-tie slots are two full 3 × 18 mm cuts rotated 90° from each other ([lines 136–137](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:136)). They form a cross through the cable hole rather than a pair of slots that traps a tie around the cable; they also turn the boss into thin fragments. Pull load transfers to the piezo solder joints.

   Fix: use two **parallel**, non-intersecting 3.5 × 7 mm slots centered roughly 5–6 mm to either side of the cable hole, then route one zip tie through them across a short heat-shrink-covered cable section. Add a 2–3 mm-high printed saddle below the hole and round/chamfer the hole entrance.

5. **Major — it floods adequately, but the acoustic arrangement is uncontrolled.** Water reaches the disc elevation: slots start at `z=3+8+4=15 mm` and end at 42 mm ([line 130](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:130)); the supported disc is at about `z=16 mm`. Six 6 mm-wide slots provide about `6×6×27 = 972 mm²` of opening, roughly 29% of the 123 mm inner circumference over that band. There is no sealed cage-air pocket.

   The problem is that a horizontal piezo has one face only 5 mm above a loose, reflective sinker and the other face open to the cage. That produces unit-to-unit-dependent loading and flexure, plus washer/cage vibration. An oil balloon also risks trapped air and a different acoustic impedance per build. It may detect sound, but two heads should not be assumed matched enough for reliable bearing estimates.

   Fix: make each head geometrically identical; immobilize the sinker; keep the disc’s water-facing surface open; and give its rear a defined backing—e.g. a shallow, sealed air/foam-backed chamber—or use a repeatable thin flexible coating rather than a variably oil-filled balloon. Mark a consistent disc orientation for both units.

6. **Minor — the top itself is a bridge-heavy print.** The cage cavity is cut up to `z=47 mm` ([line 127](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:127)); the 3 mm top/spokes begin over that void ([lines 131–133](/Users/mallhw/.claude/jobs/c05f918e/tmp/HackMIT/hardware/buoy-v2/buoy_v2.scad:131)). The spokes will bridge roughly 14 mm from wall toward the center. That is plausible on a tuned printer, but sag here further weakens the already compromised cable/eye region.

   Fix: either add three slender vertical ribs under the spoke roots, or print the top/eye as a separate cap and mechanically join it after installing the piezo and sinker.
